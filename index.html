Why this exists

After: the same tree in EchoStruct. Move with the arrow keys and hear, for example, 6. Right child of 3. Depth 2. Left child 4, right child 7. A tone tells you how deep you are, and ticks in your left and right ears tell you which children exist.

Credits and limitations

Built with TypeScript and Vite. Sounds are generated live with the Web Audio API; there are no audio files. No other libraries run in the page. The built-in voice uses your browser's speech synthesis. Example values are made up. Development was assisted by Claude, an AI assistant.

  • Covers linked lists, stacks and binary search trees only. No graphs, heaps or tree deletion yet.
  • The keyboard model was tested with keyboard-only use and automated accessibility checks, not yet with blind students.
  • Sound needs headphones or stereo speakers to hear left and right.
  • Progress is not saved between visits. Settings are.

Focus the explorer first (Tab to it, or click it). Screen reader users: the explorer passes keys straight through. On a phone or tablet without a keyboard, use the move buttons under the explorer instead.

What the sounds mean

  • Pitch is position. Tree: the root is highest and each level down is lower. Stack: the top is highest. List: pitch rises from head to tail.
  • Left and right ear. In a tree, nodes further left in sorted order sound further left.
  • Ticks after a tree node: a tick in the left ear means it has a left child, a tick in the right ear means a right child. No ticks: a leaf. In a list, a right tick means there is a next node.
  • Airy falling sound: an empty slot (practice insert questions only).
  • Low thud: there is nothing in that direction.
  • Rising two notes: correct. Falling two notes: try again.
